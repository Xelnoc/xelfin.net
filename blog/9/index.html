Why we fail to be Chronically Offline.


This is embarrassing for me to admit, but my average screen time on my phone this week was 7hrs 45min.
What's worse is that that isn't abnormal, and that mine likely isn't dissimilar to yours. The average person will have spent 2457 hours on their phone in 2025. In a world where our time is dominated by these devices, it isn't surprising to find that the natural reaction to this is the growing 'chronically offline' movement, and that people are rejecting this lifestyle to be more social and in-touch with the real world.

As someone who considers themselves to be chronically online, I have, like you, taken interest in these communities, and attempted to 'detox' from the internet. I turned off notifications on my phone. I deleted social media. I blocked it in my browser. The first time I did this, I was back on social media within 7 hours, with cravings not dissimilar to those addiction victims suffer when trying to 'cold turkey' quit. I simply couldn't take it. This deeply disturbed me, and it should deeply disturb you if you are anything like me. It was clear that I was simply hooked to social media.
